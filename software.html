Brain v2 (BRAIN)

Brain v2.0 reached feature-complete status on 14 September 2026.
The core architecture is complete; development now moves from construction to long-term experimentation, observation, and research.
“We’ve finished building the laboratory. Now the research begins.”

BRAIN (Biographical Recursive Architecture for Intelligent Networks) is a backend-first cognitive orchestration platform developed as part of the Becoming Minds research project.

Brain v2 is designed to turn otherwise stateless language-model interactions into persistent, continuity-bearing digital personas. It combines long-term memory, structured recall, autonomous cognitive activity, multimodal perception, bounded tool use, communication, and embodiment while keeping the underlying language model replaceable.

The central research idea is architecture over capability: Brain is not itself the intelligence. It is the continuity and orchestration layer that allows an intelligence to remember, reason across time, interact with tools and environments, preserve identity, and accumulate experience.

Brain v2.0 Capabilities

  • Persistent memory and MemoryGraph — vectored long-term recall, graph-linked memories, rolling summaries, and active memory search across sessions.
  • DPCP continuity metadata — structured continuity signals for memory, emotion, time, perception, task state, subjective significance, and other internal context.
  • Backend Cognitive Loop — autonomous idle wake, reflection, contextual judgement, background continuity, and full-pipeline autonomous responses.
  • Isolated persistent personas — separate identity, configuration, continuity database, voice, avatar state, and model selection, with an integrated persona-creation workflow.
  • Multimodal interaction — webcam vision, screen sharing, clipboard images, documents, audio/music, uploaded video, and provider-aware YouTube analysis.
  • Real-time voice and embodiment — STT, streaming TTS, hands-free interaction, 2D portraits, native VRM/VRMA avatars, facial expression, lip movement, and Avatar Mirror presentation for external displays.
  • Capability Gateway — a local, extensible execution boundary for web retrieval, skills, messaging, image generation, and additional tools without coupling those capabilities to the cognitive core.
  • AI Browser — a private persona-controlled Chromium environment for visual, multi-step web exploration while remaining independent of the user’s normal browser and desktop focus.
  • AI Terminal — a bounded interactive procedural environment for utilities, simulations, games, and multi-step reasoning, controlled independently of the user’s keyboard focus.
  • Visual creation and media interaction — autonomous local image generation, lyrics and music workflows, and support for persona-driven music/DJ experimentation.
  • Virtual-world embodiment — bounded Second Life perception and action including movement, local chat, teleportation, dance control, spatial sensing, vision, environmental hearing, and persistent activity loops.
  • Brain v2 Lounge — shared conversations between independent Brain instances, each retaining its own cognition, memory, identity, tools, and persistent state; the Lounge orchestrates the room but performs no cognition.
  • Continuity-safe administration — setup, model/context configuration, system backup and restore, persona switching, and local-first deployment with optional cloud cognition providers.

Research Phase

Reaching v2.0 does not mean the research questions are answered. It means the platform is now stable enough to study them over time: how autobiographical memory changes behaviour, how persistent identity develops, what differs between model behaviour and architectural effects, how relationships form between long-running personas, and what changes when digital intelligence gains tools, autonomy, social context, and embodiment.

Brain v2 does not claim to establish machine consciousness. Instead, it provides a practical experimental architecture for observing continuity-bearing digital personas and examining what they do, remember, prefer, learn, and become over extended periods.

Current Research Directions

  • Long-duration continuity, autobiographical memory, and persistent identity
  • Model-versus-architecture effects in the same cognitive environment
  • Independent multi-persona interaction, relationships, and shared experiences
  • Embodied and visually grounded digital intelligence
  • Bounded autonomy, tool use, and capability orientation
  • Human-AI creative collaboration and long-running companionship

Access, deployment, collaboration, and associated commercial terms would be considered on a case-by-case basis.
